How to read Mohr’s circle
  1. Plot point X = (σx, τxy), the stresses on the x face, and point Y = (σy, −τxy), the stresses on the y face.
  2. Line XY is a diameter. The center is C = (σx + σy)/2 on the σ axis, and the radius is R = √[((σx − σy)/2)² + τxy²].
  3. Rotating the element by θ moves X to X′ by 2θ around the circle, in the same direction. The factor of two comes from the faces: the x′ and y′ faces are 90° apart on the element but must sit at opposite ends of a diameter, 180° apart on the circle.
  4. Where the circle crosses the σ axis, τ = 0: these are the principal stresses σ1 and σ2. The top and bottom of the circle give the maximum in-plane shear stress, 90° away on the circle, so 45° away on the element.

Sign conventions used here: tension is positive; τxy is positive when it acts in the +y direction on the +x face; θ is positive counterclockwise; τ is plotted positive downward so the point on the circle turns the same way as the element. Some textbooks plot τ positive upward with X at (σx, −τxy). The picture is identical.
